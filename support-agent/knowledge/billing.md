# Plans, credits, and payments

## Plans

There are three plans: **free**, **builder**, and **pro**, in that order.

A plan is a bundle of allowances — how many projects you can keep, how many
deployments you can run, how many preview links you can share, how many AI
credits you get each month, and whether folder upload and community publishing
are switched on for you.

The exact numbers differ per plan and are not listed in this document. Look up
the specific plan to get its real limits, rather than guessing from the plan's
name. An allowance of zero means the feature is simply not available on that
plan, and the fix is to move to a higher plan — not to wait, not to retry, and
not to contact support.

Moving up a plan takes effect immediately and the new allowances apply straight
away. Moving down takes effect at the end of the paid period.

## Credits

Credits are the unit for AI usage. Anything that calls a model on your behalf —
generating code, editing a file, answering in the assistant — spends credits in
proportion to how much work the model did.

Every plan includes a monthly credit allowance that resets at the start of each
billing period. Unused credits do not roll over. When the allowance runs out, AI
features stop until the next reset or until more credits are added; the rest of
the platform — editing, previews, existing deployments — keeps working normally.

Credits are only about AI usage. Projects, previews, and deployments are limited
by plan allowances, not by credits. Running out of credits never takes a
deployment down.

## Payments and invoices

Paid plans are charged once per billing period to the card on file. Every charge
produces an invoice with an invoice number, and the payment is recorded as one
of:

- **paid** — the charge succeeded and the plan is active.
- **failed** — the charge was declined. The plan stays active through a short
  grace period while the charge is retried. If it keeps failing, the account
  drops to free, which can put it over the free plan's allowances.
- **refunded** — the charge was reversed and the money has been returned.

A failed payment is almost always the card: expired, out of funds, or blocked by
the bank. Updating the card on file and retrying the charge resolves it. Free
accounts are never charged and so never have payments on record.
